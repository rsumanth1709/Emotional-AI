# Sleep Hygiene and Circadian Rest Protocols

## The Biological Function of Sleep in Emotional Health
Sleep is an active neurobiological state essential for synaptic homeostasis, memory consolidation, and emotional processing. During Rapid Eye Movement (REM) sleep, the brain processes emotionally charged autobiographical memories while downregulating adrenergic neurochemistry. Chronic sleep deprivation elevates baseline amygdala reactivity by up to 60%, severely impairing emotional distress tolerance and increasing susceptibility to anxiety and depressive episodes.

## Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) Principles
CBT-I represents the gold-standard first-line intervention for chronic insomnia and pre-sleep hyper-arousal:
1. **Stimulus Control**: Condition the brain to associate the bed exclusively with sleep and intimacy. If unable to fall asleep after approximately 20 minutes, leave the bedroom, sit in a dimly lit room, and engage in a calming, screen-free activity until genuine sleepiness occurs.
2. **Circadian Regularity**: Anchor the circadian pacemaker by maintaining a strict, consistent wake-up time 7 days a week, regardless of total sleep duration the preceding night. Exposure to bright morning sunlight within 30 minutes of waking suppresses melatonin and resets the 24-hour suprachiasmatic nucleus clock.
3. **Thermal Regulation**: Core body temperature naturally drops by approximately 1°C during the sleep onset phase. Keeping the bedroom ambient temperature cool (between 18°C and 20°C / 65°F and 68°F) facilitates this biological thermoregulatory transition.
4. **The Bedtime Worry Vault**: Writing down unresolved tasks, anxieties, or tomorrow's agenda 2 hours before bed transfers cognitive burdens out of working memory, preventing nocturnal rumination. Avoid screens emitting short-wavelength blue light within 60 minutes of bedtime.
