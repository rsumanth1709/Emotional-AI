# Understanding and Managing Stress and Anxiety

## What is Stress vs. Anxiety?
Stress is generally a response to an external cause, such as an upcoming exam, work deadline, or conflict with a loved one. It usually subsides once the situation is resolved. In contrast, anxiety is a person's specific internal reaction to stress; its hallmark is a persistent, non-resolving feeling of apprehension or dread in situations that are not actually threatening. Both stress and anxiety trigger the body's autonomic fight-or-flight response, releasing cortisol and adrenaline, causing elevated heart rate, shallow breathing, muscle tension, and restlessness.

## Physiological Mechanisms of the Stress Response
When an individual perceives a challenge or threat, the amygdala signals the hypothalamus, which activates the sympathetic nervous system. The adrenal glands release epinephrine and norepinephrine into the bloodstream. Physical manifestations include increased respiratory rate, perspiration, peripheral blood vessel constriction, and heightened alert states. While acute stress can enhance focus for short intervals, prolonged chronic stress impairs prefrontal cognitive control, disrupts immune regulation, and exacerbates emotional reactivity.

## Evidence-Based Interventions for Stress and Anxiety
1. **Autonomic Reset Through Controlled Respiration**: Slow, diaphragmatic breathing activates the vagus nerve and stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system (the body's rest-and-digest mechanism). Engaging in a 4-second inhale followed by a 6-second exhale lowers systemic blood pressure and slows heart rate.
2. **Cognitive Time-Chunking and Boundary Setting**: When experiencing task overwhelm, breaking responsibilities into 15 to 25-minute discrete blocks (such as the Pomodoro rhythm) decreases cognitive friction. Focus on completing a single unit before considering the wider project.
3. **Physical Somatic Discharge**: Engaging in 10 to 20 minutes of moderate cardiovascular movement (such as brisk walking or stretching) metabolizes accumulated stress hormones and reduces physiological muscle tension.
4. **Structured Worry Time**: Allocating a dedicated 15-minute window in the late afternoon to write down current concerns and categorize them into actionable versus uncontrollable helps prevent intrusive rumination throughout the day.
